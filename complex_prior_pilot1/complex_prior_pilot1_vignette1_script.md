# **Complexity priors vignette script** 

## **Design**

Three-factor between-participants design.

| Cond | Order | Complexity | Evidence | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 | Stage 4 | Stage 5 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Evidence first | Low | Heat | Common planet | LC planet | Explanation 1 | Evidence-Heat | Explanation 2 |
| 2 | Evidence first | Low | Pigment | Common planet | LC planet | Explanation 1 | Evidence-Pigment | Explanation 2 |
| 3 | Evidence first | High | Heat | Common planet | HC planet | Explanation 1 | Evidence-Heat | Explanation 2 |
| 4 | Evidence first | High | Pigment | Common planet | HC planet | Explanation 1 | Evidence-Pigment | Explanation 2 |
| 5 | Elaboration first | Low | Heat | Common planet | LC planet | Explanation 1 | Explanation 2 | Evidence-Heat |
| 6 | Elaboration first | Low | Pigment | Common planet | LC planet | Explanation 1 | Explanation 2 | Evidence-Pigment |
| 7 | Elaboration first | High | Heat | Common planet | HC planet | Explanation 1 | Explanation 2 | Evidence-Heat |
| 8 | Elaboration first | High | Pigment | Common planet | HC planet | Explanation 1 | Explanation 2 | Evidence-Pigment |

In the question tables below, Pilot marks pilot-only questions and Main marks questions included in the main study.

Underlined parts are manipulated independently (50% per version). Underlined text, which highlights the surprise, is displayed only half of the time. Underlined figure notes mark figures that have two versions, and the version shown is randomized.

## **Stage 1: Common planet introduction (all conditions)**

Scientists are planning a place for people to live on an alien planet. The planet has water, air, and some small living things called organisms. 

Scientists have closely studied the planet and its living things and understand them very well.

*Figure: vignette1\_img1\_planet\_scene.png*

## **Stage 2: Complexity manipulation (Factor 2\)**

### **Low-complexity condition (cond 1, 2, 5, 6\)**

Scientists found that biological processes (the ways living things work) on this planet are usually simple.

These processes usually involve a few chemical reactions and only a few factors that affect what happens. The factors affect each other in limited ways. The effect of one factor usually stays the same, even when other factors change.

Scientists have studied these processes in detail for many years. They understand them very well.

*Figure: vignette1\_img2\_low\_complex.png*

### **High-complexity condition (cond 3, 4, 7, 8\)**

Scientists found that biological processes (the ways living things work) on this planet are usually complex.

These processes usually involve many chemical reactions and many different factors that affect what happens. The factors affect each other in complicated ways. The effect of one factor often changes when other factors change.

Scientists have studied these processes in detail for many years. They understand them very well.

*Figure: vignette1\_img2\_high\_complex.png*

### **Stage 2 questions**

| Use | Measure | Question |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Main | Perceived complexity of biological processes | Overall, how simple or complex are the biological processes on this planet? (1 \= Very simple; 7 \= Very complex) |
| Pilot | Perceived complexity of biological processes | How many different factors usually affect a biological process on this planet? (1 \= Very few; 7 \= Very many) |
| Pilot | Perceived complexity of biological processes | How much does the effect of one factor change when other factors change? (1 \= Not at all; 7 \= Very much) |
| Main | Perceived scientific understanding | How well do scientists understand the biological processes on this planet? (1 \= Not well at all; 7 \= Very well) |
| Pilot | Perceived scientific understanding | How well do scientists understand which factors affect these processes? (1 \= Not well at all; 7 \= Very well) |
| Pilot | Perceived scientific understanding | How well do scientists understand how these factors affect one another? (1 \= Not well at all; 7 \= Very well) |

## **Stage 3: Common explanation 1 (all conditions)**

A construction team is building apartments on this planet. The construction team needs technology that can produce heat to keep apartments warm. The planet has four seasons: warm, cool, moderately cold, and extremely cold. 

An energy company suggests one of their technologies that uses a small organism found on the planet. Scientists found that this organism is typical of other organisms on the planet in terms of how simple or complex it is. The company understands very well how the organism works because it has closely studied the organism with scientists.

The company explains the process that the technology relies on. In this process, the organism uses a certain gas from the air to produce heat and a purple substance called a pigment. More pigment makes the organism look more purple. The pigment is harmless and has no known practical use. When more of this gas is available, the organism produces more heat and more purple pigment.

The company also explains that the planet's air holds more of this gas when the weather is colder. So in colder weather, there is more gas around the organism.

*Figure: vignette1\_img3\_explanation1.png*

### **Stage 3 potential measurements**

* Whether participants encode the goal of the construction team.  
* Whether participants recognize the company as knowledgeable.  
* Whether participants understand the initial explanation, including the relationship between the weather and the gas.  
* Whether participants use the explanation to expect increasing heat and pigment production.  
* Whether the planet's complexity framing carries over to this particular organism.

### **Stage 3 questions**

| Use | Measure | Question |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Main | Heat prediction (cond 1, 2, 3, 4 only) | How much heat would you expect the organism to produce when it is extremely cold, compared with when it is moderately cold? (1 \= Much less heat; 4 \= About the same amount of heat; 7 \= Much more heat) |
| Main | Pigment prediction (cond 1, 2, 3, 4 only) | How much purple pigment would you expect the organism to produce when it is extremely cold, compared with when it is moderately cold? (1 \= Much less purple pigment; 4 \= About the same amount of purple pigment; 7 \= Much more purple pigment) |
| Main | Construction team's goal | What is the construction team's main reason for considering the technology? (A. To keep apartments warm in cold weather; B. To produce purple pigment; C. To study how the organism changes color; D. To increase the amount of gas in the air; E. I don't remember) |
| Pilot | Relevance of heat | How important is the amount of heat produced for the team's goal of keeping apartments warm? (1 \= Not at all important; 7 \= Extremely important) |
| Pilot | Relevance of pigment | How important is the amount of purple pigment produced for the team's goal of keeping apartments warm? (1 \= Not at all important; 7 \= Extremely important) |
| Pilot | Causal knowledge: weather and gas | According to the company, what happens to the amount of the gas in the air when the weather gets colder? (A. There is less gas; B. There is about the same amount of gas; C. There is more gas; D. I don't remember) |

| Pilot | Complexity of the organism's processes | How likely is it that the company's explanation left out other details about how the organism works? (1 \= Very unlikely; 7 \= Very likely) |

## **Stage 4, evidence-first conditions (cond 1, 2, 3, 4): Evidence**

Before deciding whether to use the technology, the construction team gets a sample of the organism from the company.

The team mainly wants to know if the organism can produce heat as explained.

The team tests the sample in a laboratory at four different temperatures. These temperatures match the average temperatures of the planet's four seasons: warm, cool, moderately cold, and extremely cold. During the tests, more of the gas is available when it is colder, just as on the planet.

### **High-goal-relevance condition (heat anomaly, cond 1, 3\)**

As it gets colder, from warm to moderately cold, the organism produces more heat and more purple pigment. <ins>This is expected.</ins>

But when it is extremely cold, the organism produces less heat than when it is moderately cold. <ins>This is unexpected, because more gas is available for the organism to use.</ins> The organism keeps producing more purple pigment<ins>, as expected</ins>.

*Figure: vignette1\_img4\_evidence\_heat\_anomaly.png* or *vignette1\_img4\_evidence\_heat\_anomaly\_line.png <ins>(randomize the two versions)</ins>.*

### **Low-goal-relevance condition (pigment anomaly, cond 2, 4\)**

As it gets colder, from warm to moderately cold, the organism produces more heat and more purple pigment. <ins>This is expected.</ins>

But when it is extremely cold, the organism produces less purple pigment than when it is moderately cold. <ins>This is unexpected, because more gas is available for the organism to use.</ins> The organism keeps producing more heat<ins>, as expected</ins>.

*Figure: vignette1\_img4\_evidence\_pigment\_anomaly.png* or *vignette1\_img4\_evidence\_pigment\_anomaly\_line.png <ins>(randomize the two versions)</ins>.*

### **Stage 4 potential measurements (evidence-first)**

* Whether participants find the evidence surprising.  
* Whether participants see the evidence as relevant to the team's goal.

### **Stage 4 questions (evidence-first)**

| Use | Measure | Question |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Main | Observed heat production | How much heat did the organism produce when it was extremely cold, compared with when it was moderately cold? (Less heat; About the same amount of heat; More heat; I don't remember) |
| Main | Observed purple pigment | How much purple pigment did the organism produce when it was extremely cold, compared with when it was moderately cold? (Less purple pigment; About the same amount of purple pigment; More purple pigment; I don't remember) |
| Pilot | Difference from expectations / Surprise | How different are these results from what you expected based on the company's explanation? (1 \= Not at all different; 7 \= Extremely different) |
| Pilot | Gas availability | How much gas was available when it was extremely cold, compared with when it was moderately cold? (Less gas; About the same amount of gas; More gas; I don't remember) |
| Pilot | Anomaly relevance, heat anomaly version (cond 1, 3\) | The organism produces less heat when it is extremely cold. How much does this matter for the team's goal of keeping apartments warm? (1 \= Not at all; 7 \= Very much) |
| Pilot | Anomaly relevance, pigment anomaly version (cond 2, 4\) | The organism produces less purple pigment when it is extremely cold. How much does this matter for the team's goal of keeping apartments warm? (1 \= Not at all; 7 \= Very much) |

## **Stage 5, evidence-first conditions (cond 1, 2, 3, 4): Elaboration**

The construction team asks the company about this unexpected result.

### **High-goal-relevance condition (heat anomaly, cond 1, 3\)**

The company explains that even smaller living things called algae live inside the organism. The organism can produce heat only with the help of the algae.

The algae need liquid water to stay active. When they are active, more gas leads them to produce more heat. When there is too little liquid water, they become less active and produce less heat.

As the weather gets colder, more gas is available in the air. When it is moderately cold, the algae use the extra gas to produce more heat.

But when it is extremely cold, most of the water available to the algae freezes. As a result, the algae become less active and produce less heat, even though more gas is available.

The organism produces purple pigment on its own, without the algae. So it keeps producing more purple pigment as the weather gets colder.

*Figure: vignette1\_img5\_explanation2\_heat\_anomaly.png*

### **Low-goal-relevance condition (pigment anomaly, cond 2, 4\)**

The company explains that even smaller living things called algae live inside the organism. The organism can produce purple pigment only with the help of the algae.

The algae need liquid water to stay active. When they are active, more gas leads them to produce more purple pigment. When there is too little liquid water, they become less active and produce less purple pigment.

As the weather gets colder, more gas is available in the air. When it is moderately cold, the algae use the extra gas to produce more purple pigment.

But when it is extremely cold, most of the water available to the algae freezes. As a result, the algae become less active and produce less purple pigment, even though more gas is available.

The organism produces heat on its own, without the algae. So it keeps producing more heat as the weather gets colder.

*Figure: vignette1\_img5\_explanation2\_pigment\_anomaly.png*
